# Cable One – Reference Notes

Nov 18, 2026 · @Sam Mchugh

## The call in sixty seconds

Own Term Loan B-4 at 83 (B-2/B-3 at 82) as a carry position; pass on the
4.00% notes at ~24, which are fairly priced; avoid the converts and
equity. These notes hold the backup behind each slide: the numbers, how
they were derived, where they come from, and the questions to expect.
The spoken talk track is in each slide's speaker notes.

- **What it is:** a liquidity crisis with a mispriced balance sheet.
  Cable One skipped a \$10m coupon on Nov 16; the grace period ends ~Dec
  15–16 and the bank debt cross-defaults then.

- **Where consensus is wrong** \[Likely\]: at today's loan prices the
  market implies ~2.8× run-rate EBITDA for a cable business. B-4 at 83
  loses money only below ~2.8× on \$580m (~3.7× if \$480m goes to MBI
  first), and the company's own plan repays it at par.

- **The swing factor:** the ~\$480m MBI payment. Cable One's own
  accounts value what it buys at ~zero; CoBank's TRO is blocking it,
  with a hearing on Dec 4.

- **The honest caveats:** only the term loans have live prices; the
  revolver, notes and converts are still Sep 30 company marks; at 83 the
  loans return ~12–16% to recovery, so this is carry, not deep value;
  ~25 points of the loans' ~93 recovery comes as equity at a 2.5× exit;
  the security agreement's excluded assets and the ranking of the GTCR
  loan are unverified.

## MBI and valuation backup (slides 9–13)

**MBI (slide 9).** \[Certain\] Cable One bought ~45% of Mega Broadband
Investments (Vyve Broadband) in November 2020. GTCR-led holders
exercised their put on Jan 2, 2026, and the purchase agreement was
signed Jan 3. The price, ~\$480m for the other ~55%, comes from a
formula on MBI's adjusted EBITDA for the twelve months to June 30, 2025
and its net debt.

- Cable One books the put as a \$478.9m current liability. The 10-Q says
  its fair value "effectively represents its intrinsic value": the price
  less the value of what is bought. On Cable One's own numbers, the 55%
  is worth ~\$1m.

- The 45% stake was impaired by \$376.4m in 2026 and is carried at zero;
  cumulative impairment is \$502.9m.

- MBI has \$916.2m of term loans due Nov 12, 2027 and \$22.0m of
  revolver due Aug 2027. They "are not required to be repaid upon any
  closing", and Cable One intends to hold MBI separate.

- MBI had ~210,000 customers and ~657,000–675,000 passings at Sep 2025
  (sources differ), and ~\$310m of revenue for the twelve months to
  September 2025.

- CoBank, a lender with ~\$1.1bn of secured exposure, got an ex parte
  TRO on Oct 9. Its complaint alleges that paying the price would be a
  constructive fraudulent transfer. It cited the Q2 put liability of
  \$425.9m as implying the stock was worth ~\$54m.

- GTCR alleges on Nov 2 that Cable One failed to use reasonable best
  efforts to fight the TRO, and has moved to intervene. Under the
  agreement Cable One need not close while a legal restraint is in
  effect.

- In October Cable One said it was near a GTCR-linked financing to
  "address \$2.45 billion" of obligations, subject to the TRO. An Oct 1
  8-K said engagement with other potential investors "has concluded".

\[Likely\] In Chapter 11, the purchase agreement looks like an executory
contract the debtor could reject, leaving GTCR with an unsecured damages
claim of up to ~\$480m, pari with the notes.

**Most likely path (slide 11).** \[Guessing\] Out-of-court GTCR
refinancing ~20–25%: B-4 repaid at par within months. Prepack with a
GTCR settlement ~50–55%: waterfall Base/Bull, fees ~\$40–80m. Contested
Chapter 11 ~20–25%: waterfall Bear/Stress, fees ~\$100–200m. Blended B-4
expected value ~94 (22.5% × 100 + 77.5% × 92.8; model, Paths tab).

Why a negotiated deal is likely but done inside Chapter 11 \[Likely\]:
(1) out of court cannot bind holdouts: extending the revolver needs each
affected lender, CoBank is suing, and curing the missed coupon by
exchange needs near-total noteholder take-up; (2) the \$2.45bn GTCR plan
does not delever: it refinances the revolver, B-4 and the put but leaves
B-2/B-3, notes and converts and adds a \$1.3bn loan, so debt stays
~\$3.8–3.9bn, ~6× falling EBITDA; (3) paying \$480m out of court for MBI
equity marked at ~\$1m invites a constructive fraudulent-transfer
challenge (§548 and state law). Chapter 11 costs are a real incentive to
agree terms first, but terms are set by each side's fallback. GTCR's
fallback is a rejected put: an unsecured damages claim pari with the
notes, worth perhaps \$50–150m at Base recoveries, plus owning MBI at
~5.6× leverage. A settlement should land at that fallback plus a share
of avoided fees: ~\$100–200m, likely in plan paper, not cash.

What the market says: B-4 trades only one point over B-2/B-3 (83 vs 82),
so the market prices the out-of-court takeout at low
single-digit-to-~20% odds, consistent with this view.

## Trade, risks and diligence backup (slides 18–20)

**Trade expression (slide 18).**

plan value and sale value is the case for holding the reorganised

- **Loans, core.** ~93 weighted (~96 with post-petition interest):

## Likely questions

1.  **Why would the company skip a \$10m coupon with ~\$675m of cash?**
    \[Likely\] Leverage. It forces the unsecured holders and GTCR to the
    table before the grace period ends, while keeping cash in the
    estate.

2.  **Why are the loans only at 82–83 if they're covered?** Partly
    off-market coupons (SOFR+200–225 for a credit in this position),
    partly MBI leakage risk, partly priming risk. A par claim earning
    ~6% marks well below par when the market wants double digits.

3.  **What's your single biggest risk?** The TRO is lifted on Dec 4 and
    \$480m leaves before a filing. That takes ~16 points of secured
    cover; with a 3.5× Bear the loans recover ~73, below their mark: the
    exit trigger.

4.  **Why not own the notes?** Zero in Bear and Stress, 41 in Base: ~29
    weighted per bond and ~23 discounted, against a 24 mark. Fairly
    priced, and ~25 points per half-turn of multiple in Base.

5.  **Isn't most of the value franchise value?** It's a large piece of
    book value. The lien reaches it: Cable One, Inc. and all 16
    subsidiaries pledge substantially all their assets, and one
    franchise expressly allows a mortgage to secure debt. Only the
    unfiled security agreement's excluded-asset list could change that.

6.  **What does "par" for the loans actually mean?** At a 2.5× exit,
    about 72% cash and new debt and 28% equity in Base. Realised value
    is ~89–100 depending on how the equity and new debt trade.

7.  **Where does GTCR rank?** Pari with the notes. Cable One, Inc., the
    issuer, signed the purchase agreement.

8.  **Why not the converts?** Same claim, ~5 points dearer, no lien
    covenant.

9.  **What would make you sell?** MBI paid in cash, run-rate EBITDA
    under \$500m, or a priming deal the loans can't block.

10. **How sure are you of the prices?** The term loans are live: B-4 83,
    B-2/B-3 82 (Nov 19), up from the 10-Q's ~78 Sep 30 mark despite the

## Deeper answers

**Two different reasons the loans can fall short of par.** First,
enterprise value can be too low: at 3.5× on \$520m, distributable value
is ~\$2.6bn against \$2.9bn of loans, so they recover ~90 even if the
lien covers everything. Second, the lien may not reach some of the
value; that part goes into a pot shared pari with the unsecured, and the
loans get only their deficiency claim's pro rata share. The main
waterfall assumes full coverage; unencumbered value is a sensitivity
only.

**Why franchise value might sit outside the lien.**

1.  A lien is a claim on specific assets; secured lenders are paid from
    those assets first.

2.  A cable franchise is a town's permission to run cable through its
    streets. Agreements and state rules often bar pledging it without
    consent, so \[Likely\] a direct lien may not be enforceable.

3.  Lenders work around this with a lien on everything else, a lien on
    the franchise's proceeds, and a pledge of the shares of the
    subsidiaries holding franchises.

4.  Checked: legacy franchises sit directly in Cable One, Inc., but
    Cable One, Inc. itself grants a lien over substantially all its
    assets, so there is no share-pledge gap (see Verification).

5.  How much of the enterprise is "franchise" is contested; \[Likely\]
    these fights usually end in a negotiated tip to the unsecured rather
    than a full win.

\[Guessing\] Only the security agreement's excluded-asset list, which is
not publicly filed, could still carve franchises out; see Verification.

**Can franchises be written down again in Chapter 11?** They can, but
write-downs don't drive recoveries. \[Certain\] The \$1.18bn of
impairments to date are accounting entries; recoveries come from the
court's valuation, and book values are reset at emergence (fresh-start
accounting). The company's own lower franchise valuation (\$920m) helps
the lenders: less value to argue is unencumbered.

**What is a DIP?** Debtor-in-possession financing: a court-approved loan
during Chapter 11 with a superpriority claim, sometimes with a lien that
primes existing secured debt if those lenders consent or are adequately
protected. \[Likely\] Existing secured lenders usually provide it to
avoid being primed, often rolling some old loans into it. With ~\$675m+
of cash and positive cash flow, Cable One may need no DIP and could
instead use its cash with lender consent, paying adequate protection.

**Bankruptcy or settlement?** The waterfall is a Chapter 11 framework.
\[Likely\] The probable path is a negotiated deal done through a
prepackaged Chapter 11, because only a filing lets Cable One reject the
MBI agreement, force holdouts on a missed coupon that needs each
noteholder's consent to waive, and deal with 2028 maturities it can't
refinance. An out-of-court extension or exchange would still be priced
off these recoveries, because Chapter 11 is each side's fallback.

**Cross-holdings and conflicted incentives** \[Guessing on who holds
what\]:

- **GTCR plays three roles:** MBI's equity owner, holder of the ~\$480m
  claim, and proposed financier of a \$2.45bn package. New money from
  GTCR that primes the loans and pays GTCR's own \$480m would mean GTCR
  lending to pay itself, with the loans bearing it.

- **Lenders to both MBI and Cable One** may prefer the MBI deal to
  close, because MBI gets a stronger owner. That is the opposite of
  CoBank's position; CoBank's own MBI exposure, if any, is unknown.

- **Lenders invited into a GTCR deal versus those left out:** a classic
  split if the credit agreement's pro-rata and sacred-rights terms allow
  it.

- **Crossover holders** of both loans and notes are softer in fights
  between the classes.

- **A lender cooperation agreement**, as at Optimum, would block most of
  these splits. Whether one exists is unknown.

## Chapter 11 process: valuation, sales and the revolver

- **Who sets value.** The plan proponent's banker proposes a range; the
  lenders argue low (they keep the equity and the upside), the unsecured
  committee argues high. Most cases settle; the judge decides only if
  the plan is contested, applying absolute priority and the
  best-interests test. A real market test (sale process, rights
  offering, competing bids) outweighs any expert DCF. \[Certain\]

- **For Cable One:** lenders will value on falling 2027E EBITDA
  (~\$460–520m → ~\$2.1–2.3bn, notes near zero); the committee will cite
  5×+ and Charter–Cox at 6.4×. A real Charter bid around 4.5× 2027E
  would effectively settle it. Plan value mostly decides how much of the
  new company B-4 owns; for the notes it decides almost everything.
  \[Likely\]

- **Stalking horse.** The first signed bidder in a §363 sale sets the
  floor, gets a break-up fee (~2–4%), expense reimbursement and matching
  rights, and an auction follows. A fully marketed bid that nobody tops
  is the strongest evidence of value. \[Certain\]

- **Credit bid (§363(k)).** Secured lenders can bid their claim instead
  of cash, up to its face, so they are often the stalking horse; cash
  rivals must top it in real money. \[Certain\]

- **Why lenders bid below their claim.** A credit bid costs no cash, but
  bidding the full claim uses all of it up. Bidding, say, \$2.2bn of
  \$2.9bn leaves a ~\$0.7bn deficiency claim that shares with the notes
  in unencumbered assets, and a low winning bid caps the juniors'
  valuation argument. \[Certain on mechanics\]

- **Why a prepack is still more likely than a sale.** There is no cash
  buyer yet (Charter is above its leverage target and integrating Cox);
  a sale leaves the MBI put, GTCR, CoBank and the notes to a plan
  anyway; a plan keeps franchises, pole and programming contracts and
  FCC licences in place without assignment; and it keeps tax attributes.
  The §363 credit bid stays in reserve as the lenders' threat if the
  committee fights the valuation. \[Likely\]

- **Open, and decisive for the notes:** whether deposit accounts are
  under control agreements (cash without them can be stripped of the
  lien under §544, and borrowed revolver cash is not proceeds of other
  collateral); the security agreement's excluded assets; whether the
  Point stake is pledged (if not excluded, the lien probably attaches
  but can't easily be enforced). None is public; check the 2023 credit
  agreement (Exhibit 10.1, Feb 22, 2023 8-K) for "Control Agreement" and
  "Excluded". \[Unknown\]

- **Why the revolver was drawn.** Each draw requires no default and a
  bring-down of the company's representations, so companies draw before
  a missed coupon or going-concern warning shuts access. Cash gives
  leverage over DIP financing, funds the case and specific payments, and
  is cheap (~SOFR+1.5%). Cable One drew \$700m on Sep 17–18, repaid
  \$200m and drew the last \$200m on Oct 8, fully drawn before its Nov 5
  warning and the Nov 16 missed coupon. \[Certain\]

- **Revolver vs B-4.** Pari passu: same credit agreement, guarantors and
  collateral, sharing pro rata. In practice the revolver matures first
  (Feb vs May 2028), the GTCR plan repays B-4 at par but only extends
  the revolver, and lending banks holding the company's deposits may be
  able to set them off. The revolver's 43% of the vote is influence, not
  priority. \[Certain on ranking; Likely on set-off\]

## Glossary

| Term                             | Meaning                                                                                             |
|----------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Pari passu                       | Ranking equally; each dollar of claim gets the same cents                                           |
| Senior unsecured                 | No lien on assets; shares what's left after secured creditors, pro rata                             |
| Effectively subordinated         | Unsecured debt behind secured debt only up to the collateral's value                                |
| Structurally subordinated        | A parent creditor behind the creditors of subsidiaries holding the assets                           |
| Deficiency claim                 | A secured lender's shortfall when collateral is worth less than its claim; ranks with the unsecured |
| Fulcrum security                 | The class that receives most of the equity in a restructuring                                       |
| Distributable value              | Enterprise value plus cash and other assets, less admin and fees                                    |
| Executory contract               | An agreement with obligations left on both sides; a debtor can reject it, leaving a damages claim   |
| Constructive fraudulent transfer | A transfer for less than fair value while insolvent; can be blocked or reversed                     |
| Safe harbour                     | Bankruptcy Code protection letting swap counterparties terminate despite the automatic stay         |
| Set-off                          | Netting what a creditor owes the debtor against what the debtor owes it                             |
| Springing maturity               | A maturity that moves earlier if another tranche is still outstanding                               |
| Sacred rights                    | Credit agreement terms that need each affected lender's consent, such as pro-rata sharing           |
| Priming                          | New debt ranked ahead of existing secured debt                                                      |
| Post-petition interest           | Interest an oversecured creditor can claim after a Chapter 11 filing                                |
