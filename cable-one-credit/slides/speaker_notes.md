# Cable One – Credit Pitch: speaker notes

## 1. Cable One: own Term Loan B-4 at 83

Open with the call, then defend it. Cable One has not filed. It skipped a $10m coupon on Nov 16 and its 30-day grace ends around Dec 15–16, at which point the bank debt cross-defaults. So this is a "paths to and through a restructuring" pitch, not a post-bankruptcy one. B-4 trades at 83 and B-2/B-3 at 82. Pricing the loans there, the revolver at a similar level and the unsecured at their Sep 30 marks, the market values the business at about 2.8x Q3 run-rate EBITDA. B-4 only loses money below about 2.8x on $580m of EBITDA, or about 3.7x if $480m goes to MBI first. At 83 this is a carry trade with a free option, not a deep-value call: about 14% simple and 22% a year on expected timing including a par takeout in the company's own plan that costs only 1 point over B-2/B-3.

## 2. Own Term Loan B-4 at 83, sized as carry; pass on the notes

One trade, two passes. At 83, B-4 recovers about 89, 100, 100 and 73 across Bear, Base, Bull and Stress: about 93 weighted in Chapter 11. Adding the 22.5% chance of a par takeout out of court and interest until a filing, about 95 expected: 14% simple and about 22% a year on the expected timing. If the reorganised debt and equity are sold at emergence at 95 and a 30% discount, that falls to about 8% a year, so this is a carry trade, not a deep-value call, and I would size it that way. What makes B-4 the right tranche is the 1-point spread to B-2/B-3: for 1 point you get a par takeout if the company's GTCR plan goes ahead, and the same Chapter 11 claim if it doesn't. The notes are fairly priced, and the converts are the same claim at a higher price. The revolver, notes and converts prices are still Sep 30 marks; only the term loan prices are live.

## 3. The 30-day clock is already running

The timeline matters because the catalyst is dated. The company has said in the 10-Q that its liquidity cannot repay the 2028 debt, and it chose to skip a $10m coupon while holding roughly $675m of cash at Sep 30. That is a negotiating signal, not a cash problem: they want the unsecured holders and GTCR at the table. Under the indenture the coupon becomes an Event of Default only after 30 days, and the credit agreement's cross-default turns on that same grace expiry, so mid-December is when the bank lenders get remedies. The TRO hearing moved from Nov 13 to Dec 4. On the maturity wall: the springing maturity on B-2/B-3 means effectively all bank debt is due in 2028 unless B-4 is dealt with.

## 4. Term loans at 82–83; unsecured marked at 24–30

Read the table bottom-up. The unsecured paper is $848m, last marked at 24 to 30 at Sep 30. The secured is $2.9bn, all pari: revolver and three term loans under one credit agreement. The term loans now trade at 82 to 83, up from the 10-Q's blended 77.8 mark at Sep 30, despite the missed coupon. B-4, which the company's own plan repays at par, trades only 1 point over B-2 and B-3. Two structural facts: first, notes and converts share guarantors with the bank debt, so the unsecured claim sits at the same entities, just behind the lien. Second, the loans pay only SOFR plus 200 to 225, which is off-market for a credit in this position, so part of the discount is coupon, not principal risk.

## 5. One secured layer, one pari unsecured layer

Rank 1: the revolver and the three term loans sit under one credit agreement, all senior secured and pari; the 10-Q discloses no first-out revolver. The swaps would rank with them if a lender holds them, but they are currently an asset to Cable One, so there is no claim. Rank 2: the notes and converts are both senior unsecured, guaranteed by the subsidiaries that guarantee the bank debt, and pari with each other; trade claims sit alongside them. If the loans were ever undersecured, their shortfall would join this pool as a deficiency claim. On GTCR: I had argued its claim would be structurally junior because it sits only at the parent. The purchase agreement says otherwise. Cable One, Inc. signed it directly, alongside its wholly owned Major Merger Sub, and Cable One, Inc. is also the borrower and the notes' issuer. So a damages claim on rejection is pari with the notes at the issuer. The notes' only edge is their subsidiary guarantees, which matter for value held below the parent; the filings I have read don't show how assets split between the parent and its subsidiaries, so I keep GTCR pari in the waterfall. MBI's own $938m of debt sits in a separate silo, not guaranteed by Cable One.

## 6. Shrinking, but still cash-generative

Run-rate free cash flow: Q3 EBITDA of $159.8m annualizes to $639m. Capex was $220m for nine months, about $295m a year. Interest was $39.4m gross in Q3; with the extra $200m revolver draw, call it $170m a year. So $639m less $295m less $170m is roughly $175m before tax. The decline is real: EBITDA down 21% year on year, revenue down 12%, and margins compressing as ARPU is used to defend subscribers. The Q3 subscriber improvement may be bought with price, so I would not underwrite a recovery in EBITDA. The point for credit is that this business does not need new money to operate; it needs its 2028 maturities dealt with.

## 7. Cable One: rural, single-family, and passings ≈ homes

Two panels from the same FCC and Census build. Panel A is the kind of home in each footprint: each operator's FCC cable and fiber locations are mapped to 2020 Census blocks, and the building mix comes from the American Community Survey, table B25024, by block group. Cable One is 74% single-family, 8% mobile homes and other, and 18% apartments, against a US average of 68%, 6% and 27%. Only Vyve, which is MBI, has fewer apartments. Astound is the other extreme at 52%. Panel B compares reported passings with Census homes, both per FCC location. Cable One reports 1.27 passings per location against 1.18 Census homes, so its passings run about 8% above homes, the gap being business sites, growth since 2020 and counting rules. Comcast, Optimum, Charter and Cox report 1.54 to 1.87 passings per location, 16 to 28% above their Census homes. WOW!, Breezeline and Astound report fewer passings than homes. Two uses for the pitch. First, Cable One's 2.8 million passings are close to a real home count, so applying a big cable company's dollar-per-passing to Cable One undervalues it per home by roughly 10%: each of their passings is fewer real homes. Second, a rural, single-family footprint is expensive for unsubsidised fiber to overbuild, but it is exactly where subsidised rural fiber goes, and management already says about 60% of passings face gig-capable competition. Fewer apartments also means less exposure to bulk deals and in-building fiber, and usually lower churn. For a buyer such as Charter, Cable One lowers density. Caveats: Census 2020 housing counts include vacant homes; the mix is applied by neighbourhood, not building; Optimum counts residential units only, and Mediacom discloses only over 3 million passings.

## 8. Rural footprint: less FWA, more Starlink

This splits each operator's FCC cable and fiber locations by the housing density of their Census block group, and shows where T-Mobile, Verizon or AT&amp;T report fixed wireless at the same location. Cable One is the most rural of the larger operators: 51% of its locations are in block groups with fewer than 500 homes per square mile and 23% under 100, against 34% and 18% for US homes, 40% and 18% for Charter, and 28% and 9% for Comcast. Only Vyve, which is MBI, and Mediacom are more rural. That cuts two ways. Fixed wireless is lighter: the carriers report 100/20 or faster fixed wireless at 37% of Cable One's locations, joint lowest with Vyve, against 43 to 52% for the urban operators, though some fixed wireless is offered at 81%. Satellite is the bigger rural risk. I use the low-density share as a proxy because Starlink sells capacity cell by cell and surcharges or waitlists congested cells, so it competes hardest where homes are sparse. For the credit, this supports valuing Cable One below urban cable peers, which the 3.5 to 6.0x range already does, and it is another reason not to pay for MBI, whose footprint is even more rural: 73% under 500 homes per square mile and 37% under 100. Caveats: fixed-wireless filings are carriers' claims of where they could install within ten business days, and satellite exposure is inferred from density, not measured.

## 9. MBI: $480m for equity marked at ~zero

This is the slide that decides the trade. The put price was fixed by formula on MBI's EBITDA for the twelve months to June 2025, before MBI's performance deteriorated. Cable One's own accounting says the payment buys almost nothing: the liability is booked at intrinsic value, and it equals the price. CoBank's fraudulent-transfer argument is therefore strong on the company's own numbers, which is why I think the TRO is more likely to hold than not. In Chapter 11 the purchase agreement looks like an executory contract the debtor can reject, which turns GTCR's claim into an unsecured damages claim. That is good for the loans and bad for the notes, and it is why my Bear and Base cases add a GTCR claim to the unsecured pool. The risk case is the TRO being dissolved on Dec 4 and the cash going out before a filing.

## 10. Settle the claim; don’t buy the equity

Separate the equity from the claim. The equity is worth roughly nothing: on about $160m of falling EBITDA and $938m of debt due November 2027, MBI's enterprise value only exceeds its debt above about 6x, and Cable One's own accounts value the 55% at about $1m. So nobody should pay for the equity. What has value is GTCR's legal claim: outside bankruptcy a court could order the close; inside it, rejection turns the claim into an unsecured one that dilutes the notes. GTCR is not desperate: it has already taken about $350m out, $250m upfront in December 2024 and $100m of new MBI debt passed through to MBI's owners. Its fallback in a Cable One bankruptcy is a contested unsecured claim, maybe $300m to $480m allowed at about 29% weighted recovery in reorganised paper, worth perhaps $50m to $150m today, plus control of an MBI whose organised lenders can take the keys. So the rational deal is a settlement of perhaps $100m to $200m of value, paid in paper or bundled with GTCR new money, in which Cable One releases the put and does not take the equity. If Cable One wants MBI's systems for the cost savings management has cited, it should buy them from MBI's lenders after they restructure. I found no evidence that GTCR holds MBI debt.

## 11. Negotiated, but through a prepack

A negotiated deal with GTCR is likely, but done through a prepackaged Chapter 11 rather than instead of one. A purely out-of-court refinancing is the minority outcome, perhaps 20 to 25%, consistent with B-4 trading only a point over B-2/B-3. Out of court, the company can't bind holdouts: extending the revolver needs each lender's consent and CoBank is suing, and fixing the missed coupon by exchange needs near-total noteholder participation. It also doesn't delever: the $2.45bn plan refinances the revolver, B-4 and the MBI put, but leaves B-2/B-3, the notes and converts in place and adds a $1.3bn loan, so debt stays around $3.8 to 3.9bn, about 6x on falling EBITDA. And paying GTCR $480m outside a court process for equity the company marks at about $1m invites a fraudulent-transfer challenge. Chapter 11 costs, perhaps $40 to 80m in a prepack and $100 to 200m contested, are a real incentive to agree terms first. But terms are set by each side's Chapter 11 fallback, and GTCR's is weak, so the settlement should land near $100 to 200m in paper. The waterfall scenarios still set Chapter 11 values; these paths set timing and fees. Blending in the out-of-court path, B-4's expected value is about 94 (see the Paths tab of the model).

## 12. Point is worth ~$55m to lenders, more to GTCR

Cable One's minority stakes are $264m of book value: Point Broadband 15% at $130.8m, Nextlink 22% at $116.0m and $17.5m of small cost-method holdings. In May 2026 Cable One contributed its 57% of Clearwave Fiber into Point for more Point equity, booking a $60.1m gain; GTCR and Berkshire control the combined company. It had over 500,000 fiber passings at closing and will reach about 900,000 once Ting's owned networks are added, expected in the first quarter of 2027. There is no public balance sheet, but Point has issued more than $1.1bn of fiber securitisation since 2025, $725m in July 2025 and $403m in August 2026, and Ting brings $301.5m of debt. So pro forma debt is about $1.4bn. At $2,000 a passing that is $1.8bn of enterprise value, $370m of equity and about $56m for Cable One's 15%, under half of book. Book value implies about $2,500 a passing, and the May transaction about $3,200. The stake is highly levered to that number: each $500 a passing is about $68m. Point at $2,000 plus Nextlink at half of book gives about $120m, the figure the waterfall now uses. The more interesting use is as currency. GTCR co-controls Point and controls MBI. Handing GTCR the Point stake in return for releasing the MBI put lets GTCR consolidate a fiber platform it already runs, and lets Cable One settle a $100 to 200m claim without cash leaving the lenders' collateral. Two things to check: whether the stake is pledged under the credit agreement, and the joint-venture transfer terms, which probably give GTCR and Berkshire first refusal anyway.

## 13. The market implies ~2.8× EBITDA

This is the "where is consensus wrong" slide. With the term loans at 82 to 83, the revolver assumed at 82, the notes and converts at their Sep 30 marks and the equity at about $30m, total claims less pro forma cash imply an EV of about $1.77bn, or about 2.8x Q3 run-rate EBITDA. Adding the MBI put liability, economically a $479m claim on Cable One, takes it to about 3.5x. Precedent cable deals printed in the mid-to-high 6x range, though for better assets; I use 3.5x in Bear, 4.5x in Base and 6.0x in Bull. The B-4 breakeven: at 83 it needs about $2.41bn of distributable value. Less about $770m of cash and stakes net of fees, that is an EV of about $1.64bn: 2.8x on $580m, 3.1x on $520m. If $480m of cash goes to MBI first, the breakeven rises to about $2.12bn, 3.7x on $580m. My Bear case, 3.5x on $520m, is $1.82bn of EV, which still clears it.

## 14. Loans mostly covered; notes mostly not

Walk the columns left to right. Multiples are 3.5x in Bear, 4.5x in Base and 6.0x in Bull, reflecting a business losing about 20% of EBITDA a year; the 6.4x to 6.8x precedents were for healthier assets. Cash at filing: $675m at Sep 30 plus the $200m October draw, less Q4 interest and fees, so $750m; in Stress the $480m MBI payment comes out of it. Minority stakes are Point (15%), Nextlink (22%) and smaller holdings, $264m of book value. Point is valued at $2,000 a passing, about $56m, and Nextlink and the rest at half of book, about $120m in all; at the May deal value of about $3,200 a passing it would be about $285m, adding about 5.5 points of secured recovery in Bear. Secured claims are the fully drawn $1.25bn revolver plus $1.65bn of term loans, all pari. The loans recover 89 in Bear, par in Base and Bull, and 73 in Stress, about 93 weighted. The unsecured get nothing in Bear or Stress and 41 in Base, about 28.5 weighted, or about 29 per bond including unpaid interest, so at 24 they are fairly priced. Each half-turn of multiple on $580m is $290m, about 25 points of unsecured recovery in Base. Post-petition interest for the loans, where oversecured, is not modelled and is upside.

## 15. “Par” for the loans comes partly in equity

Exit leverage has to be below 3x, ideally 2.5x, for a business losing around 20% of EBITDA a year. At that leverage the secured lenders get the cash on hand, about $650m after fees, plus all the new debt, and take the rest of their claim in equity valued at the plan multiple. With 3.5x, 4.5x and 6.0x valuations, the loans recover 89 in Bear, par in Base and Bull, and 73 in Stress; weighted, about 25 of those points are equity at a 2.5x exit. Losing the coupon does not destroy value: interest falls to about $125m a year on $1.45bn of new debt in Base, and that cash flow accrues to the equity, which the loans largely own. What it changes is realised return: with new debt at 95 and equity 30% below plan value, Base realises about 89. The offset is control: the loans own 62% of the new company in Base and all of it in Bear and Stress, and a sub-scale rural cable operator is a natural sale candidate.

## 16. 2.5× only works if the decline slows

About 25 points of B-4's recovery is equity in the reorganised company, so the plan value only holds if the new company can carry its debt. In Base, at 2.5x on $580m, the new first-lien debt is $1.45bn. At SOFR of 3.92% plus 450 basis points, interest is about $122m, 4.8x covered. After about $294m of capex at the current run-rate and about $30m of cash taxes, free cash flow is about $134m, 9% of the debt. If all of it repays debt, leverage edges down to 2.3x if EBITDA falls 5% a year, drifts to 3.0x by year three at minus 10%, and reaches 5.0x at today's 20% pace. So 2.5x is only sustainable if the decline slows, which is the case for lower multiples and for not going above 2.5x. The debt that stays as debt is unlikely to keep its terms. Reinstatement would keep the cheap SOFR+200–225 coupons, but it applies to the whole claim, and $2.9bn at about 5x isn't viable. A negotiated plan gives new take-back loans, perhaps five to seven years at SOFR+400–500. In a cramdown the court can impose a rate near prime plus 1 to 3% with a stretched maturity, paper that can trade below par. Cash taxes may be higher than assumed, because cancellation-of-debt income, though excluded in bankruptcy, reduces tax attributes.

## 17. A ~$57m asset the banks may keep

The swaps pay fixed at about 2.6% against SOFR, so with SOFR higher they are in the money to Cable One: a $57m asset at Sep 30, saving about $13m a year in interest. In a default the mechanics run through the ISDA, which I have not read. Before a filing the counterparty can treat the notes default as a cross-default once it ripens and either terminate or suspend payments under Section 2(a)(iii); US courts have not let a counterparty withhold indefinitely after a bankruptcy, so in practice it terminates. Swaps are safe-harboured, so termination is allowed despite the stay. Because the swap is an asset to Cable One, the counterparty owes the termination value. If, as is typical, the counterparty is a lender whose hedge is secured under the credit agreement, it will set off that amount against its loan claim. For our trade that means at most about $57m, two points of secured cover, and it may never reach the estate as cash. Don't count the lost hedge twice: the termination value is the present value of the interest savings, so once the swaps are settled the company is economically whole. In Chapter 11, undersecured lenders get no post-petition interest, and at emergence the old debt and its hedges are replaced. Cable One can also terminate at its own election, which would turn the asset into roughly $57m of cash before a filing.

## 18. B-4 for carry; the notes are fairly priced

Returns are now time-weighted. Per 100 of B-4 bought at 83, the expected cash flows are: interest until an assumed December 16 filing; a 22.5% chance of par plus interest from an out-of-court GTCR refinancing in February 2027; and the Chapter 11 waterfall value, about 93 weighted, received in a prepack in June 2027 or a contested case in February 2028. That is about 95 expected, a 14% simple return and about 22% a year on an XIRR basis, or 27% a year if the loans also collect post-petition interest. The catch is that the Chapter 11 value is plan value: about 48 points of it is new debt and 25 is new equity. If you sell the new debt at 95 and the new equity at a 30% discount at emergence, you realise about 87, a 5% simple return and about 8% a year. So this is a carry position whose upside depends on holding the reorganised equity, not a conviction long. B-2 and B-3 at 82 show a slightly higher return to the same Chapter 11 recovery, but B-4 is the better buy: for 1 point you also get par if the company's GTCR plan goes ahead. If B-2/B-3 would be worth 90 to 95 under that plan, a 1-point spread implies the market gives it only 10 to 20% odds. That's the cheap option in this structure. The notes, at the 24.3 September mark, would earn about 28% a year to an expected 30 per bond, but they were around 28 on October 2, which cuts that to about 7%; with nothing in Bear or Stress, that is a pass. Two interest points cut both ways for the notes. Their claim includes about $12m of unpaid interest at a mid-December filing, about 102 per 100 face, and in default they trade flat, so a buyer pays nothing for it. But the loans' post-petition interest, where they are oversecured, is paid ahead of the notes: if the loans collect their 6 points, the notes' weighted recovery falls from about 29 to about 22. The converts are the same claim, 5 points dearer, without the lien covenant. The revolver, notes and converts prices are Sep 30 marks; I would want live quotes before acting on them.

## 19. Five dates, one risk that matters

The catalysts are dated and close together, which is what makes this tradeable now. The single risk that matters for the loans is the MBI cash. If the TRO is dissolved on Dec 4 and the company pays GTCR before filing, about $480m leaves the estate, and with a 3.5x Bear multiple the loans recover about 73, below their 83 price: that is the exit trigger. The second risk is a priming transaction. Because the company says it is negotiating a GTCR financing to address $2.45bn of obligations, I want to read the credit agreement's pro-rata and sacred-rights provisions and know whether CoBank, with roughly $1.1bn, can block a Required Lender vote. The decline risk is real for the notes, but B-4 at 83 only loses money if EV falls below about $1.6bn, about 3.1x on $520m of EBITDA. The priming risk is now evidenced, not theoretical. The credit agreement's sacred rights protect pro-rata sharing but do not list lien or payment subordination, and the company's June exchange offer already drafted a first-out and second-out structure. Its October plan with GTCR was agreed with all revolving lenders except CoBank, repays B-4 in full and adds a new $1.3bn loan of undisclosed ranking, so B-2 and B-3 holders are the ones who could be left behind. Unencumbered franchise value is now a low risk: Cable One, Inc. and all sixteen subsidiaries are loan parties pledging substantially all assets. Legacy franchises sit in Cable One, Inc. itself, acquired ones in subsidiaries, and one Hargray franchise expressly allows a mortgage to secure debt. Only the unfiled security agreement's excluded-asset list could still carve them out.

## 20. What’s proven, and what I’d still check

This slide separates what I have proved from the filings from what is only partly answered. Proven: the borrower and all sixteen subsidiaries pledge substantially all assets, and a payment default on the notes cross-defaults the bank debt and converts once the 30 days run. The credit agreement's sacred rights in 9.02(b) cover increasing commitments, cutting principal, rate or fees, extending maturities, the pro rata sharing in 2.17(b) and (c), the voting definitions, and releasing all or substantially all guarantees or collateral. They do not protect against subordinating the liens or payment. Required Lenders is more than 50% of credit exposure and unused commitments. B-2 and B-3 are $959m, about 33% of the $2.9bn, so they cannot block a Required Lender amendment on their own; the revolver banks and B-4 holders together are about 67%. And section 2.10(d) allows individually negotiated discounted prepayments of term loans, so the company can buy out chosen lenders below par without sharing pro rata. That is the route by which B-2 and B-3 could be left behind. The revolver lenders in the March 2025 amendment are JPMorgan as agent, Bank of America, CoBank, Truist, Wells Fargo, U.S. Bank, TD, Citizens, Fifth Third and MUFG. Partly answered: Bloomberg reported on October 8 a roughly $1.25bn first-lien term loan from existing lenders and new investors to pay down secured debt, with no priming reported; the November 16 8-K says the company is still pursuing new debt financing. Creditor groups have organised across the stack since July, but none is named publicly. The notes were around 28 on October 2, before the missed coupon. The 10-Q does not name the swap counterparties and says either side can terminate. Still open: the security agreement, which is not publicly filed, so whether the Point stake is pledged; live revolver and convert prices; and the Point transfer terms.
