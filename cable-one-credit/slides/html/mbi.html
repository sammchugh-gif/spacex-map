The MBI knot

MBI: $480m for equity marked at ~zero

  • Cable One owns 45% of MBI. GTCR exercised its put in January: ~$480m for the other 55%.
  • Cable One books the put as a $478.9m liability at intrinsic value: on its own marks, the 55% it must buy is worth about $1m.
  • The 45% stake was written down by $376m in 2026, to zero.
  • MBI has $938m of its own debt, $916m due Nov 2027, not guaranteed by Cable One.
  • CoBank (~$1.1bn of secured exposure) won a TRO calling the payment a constructive fraudulent transfer. GTCR alleges breach and wants in.

Three ways it ends

Blocked, then rejected in Chapter 11

No cash out; Cable One never takes the equity. GTCR gets an unsecured claim of up to ~$480m.

TRO lifted, deal closes

$480m leaves the collateral: ~16 points of cover. Likely clawable as a fraudulent transfer.

Folded into a GTCR financing

The company cited a GTCR package for $2.45bn of obligations. Value depends on whether new money primes.

Cable One · credit view · Sources: Q3 10-Q notes 5, 10, 17; Fierce Network; Inside Towers

This is the slide that decides the trade. The put price was fixed by formula on MBI's EBITDA for the twelve months to June 2025, before MBI's performance deteriorated. Cable One's own accounting says the payment buys almost nothing: the liability is booked at intrinsic value, and it equals the price. CoBank's fraudulent-transfer argument is therefore strong on the company's own numbers, which is why I think the TRO is more likely to hold than not. In Chapter 11 the purchase agreement looks like an executory contract the debtor can reject, which turns GTCR's claim into an unsecured damages claim. That is good for the loans and bad for the notes, and it is why my Bear and Base cases add a GTCR claim to the unsecured pool. The risk case is the TRO being dissolved on Dec 4 and the cash going out before a filing.
