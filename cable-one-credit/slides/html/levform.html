Cable One · credit view · Source: own estimates on Q3 10-Q balances

Exit leverage has to be below 3x, ideally 2.5x, for a business losing around 20% of EBITDA a year. At that leverage the secured lenders get the cash on hand, about $650m after fees, plus all the new debt, and take the rest of their claim in equity valued at the plan multiple. With 3.5x, 4.5x and 6.0x valuations, the loans recover 89 in Bear, par in Base and Bull, and 73 in Stress; weighted, about 25 of those points are equity at a 2.5x exit. Losing the coupon does not destroy value: interest falls to about $125m a year on $1.45bn of new debt in Base, and that cash flow accrues to the equity, which the loans largely own. What it changes is realised return: with new debt at 95 and equity 30% below plan value, Base realises about 89. The offset is control: the loans own 62% of the new company in Base and all of it in Bear and Stress, and a sub-scale rural cable operator is a natural sale candidate.
