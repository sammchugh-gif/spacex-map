Returns are now time-weighted. Per 100 of B-4 bought at 83, the expected cash flows are: interest until an assumed December 16 filing; a 22.5% chance of par plus interest from an out-of-court GTCR refinancing in February 2027; and the Chapter 11 waterfall value, about 93 weighted, received in a prepack in June 2027 or a contested case in February 2028. That is about 95 expected, a 14% simple return and about 22% a year on an XIRR basis, or 27% a year if the loans also collect post-petition interest. The catch is that the Chapter 11 value is plan value: about 48 points of it is new debt and 25 is new equity. If you sell the new debt at 95 and the new equity at a 30% discount at emergence, you realise about 87, a 5% simple return and about 8% a year. So this is a carry position whose upside depends on holding the reorganised equity, not a conviction long. B-2 and B-3 at 82 show a slightly higher return to the same Chapter 11 recovery, but B-4 is the better buy: for 1 point you also get par if the company's GTCR plan goes ahead. If B-2/B-3 would be worth 90 to 95 under that plan, a 1-point spread implies the market gives it only 10 to 20% odds. That's the cheap option in this structure. The notes, at the 24.3 September mark, would earn about 28% a year to an expected 30 per bond, but they were around 28 on October 2, which cuts that to about 7%; with nothing in Bear or Stress, that is a pass. Two interest points cut both ways for the notes. Their claim includes about $12m of unpaid interest at a mid-December filing, about 102 per 100 face, and in default they trade flat, so a buyer pays nothing for it. But the loans' post-petition interest, where they are oversecured, is paid ahead of the notes: if the loans collect their 6 points, the notes' weighted recovery falls from about 29 to about 22. The converts are the same claim, 5 points dearer, without the lien covenant. The revolver, notes and converts prices are Sep 30 marks; I would want live quotes before acting on them.
