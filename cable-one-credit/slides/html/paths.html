Why not out of court [Likely]

  • Holdouts can’t be bound. Extending the revolver needs each lender; CoBank is suing. Curing the coupon by exchange needs near-total noteholder take-up.
  • It doesn’t delever. The $2.45bn plan leaves B-2/B-3, notes and converts in place and adds a $1.3bn loan: debt stays ~$3.8–3.9bn, ~6× falling EBITDA.
  • Paying GTCR is challengeable. $480m for equity marked at ~$1m invites a fraudulent-transfer suit.
  • Chapter 11 costs push everyone to agree terms before filing, at GTCR’s weak Chapter 11 fallback.

Cable One · credit view · Sources: company release; Light Reading; own estimates

A negotiated deal with GTCR is likely, but done through a prepackaged Chapter 11 rather than instead of one. A purely out-of-court refinancing is the minority outcome, perhaps 20 to 25%, consistent with B-4 trading only a point over B-2/B-3. Out of court, the company can't bind holdouts: extending the revolver needs each lender's consent and CoBank is suing, and fixing the missed coupon by exchange needs near-total noteholder participation. It also doesn't delever: the $2.45bn plan refinances the revolver, B-4 and the MBI put, but leaves B-2/B-3, the notes and converts in place and adds a $1.3bn loan, so debt stays around $3.8 to 3.9bn, about 6x on falling EBITDA. And paying GTCR $480m outside a court process for equity the company marks at about $1m invites a fraudulent-transfer challenge. Chapter 11 costs, perhaps $40 to 80m in a prepack and $100 to 200m contested, are a real incentive to agree terms first. But terms are set by each side's Chapter 11 fallback, and GTCR's is weak, so the settlement should land near $100 to 200m in paper. The waterfall scenarios still set Chapter 11 values; these paths set timing and fees. Blending in the out-of-court path, B-4's expected value is about 94 (see the Paths tab of the model).
