About 25 points of B-4's recovery is equity in the reorganised company, so the plan value only holds if the new company can carry its debt. In Base, at 2.5x on $580m, the new first-lien debt is $1.45bn. At SOFR of 3.92% plus 450 basis points, interest is about $122m, 4.8x covered. After about $294m of capex at the current run-rate and about $30m of cash taxes, free cash flow is about $134m, 9% of the debt. If all of it repays debt, leverage edges down to 2.3x if EBITDA falls 5% a year, drifts to 3.0x by year three at minus 10%, and reaches 5.0x at today's 20% pace. So 2.5x is only sustainable if the decline slows, which is the case for lower multiples and for not going above 2.5x. The debt that stays as debt is unlikely to keep its terms. Reinstatement would keep the cheap SOFR+200–225 coupons, but it applies to the whole claim, and $2.9bn at about 5x isn't viable. A negotiated plan gives new take-back loans, perhaps five to seven years at SOFR+400–500. In a cramdown the court can impose a rate near prime plus 1 to 3% with a stretched maturity, paper that can trade below par. Cash taxes may be higher than assumed, because cancellation-of-debt income, though excluded in bankruptcy, reduces tax attributes.
