This slide separates what I have proved from the filings from what is only partly answered. Proven: the borrower and all sixteen subsidiaries pledge substantially all assets, and a payment default on the notes cross-defaults the bank debt and converts once the 30 days run. The credit agreement's sacred rights in 9.02(b) cover increasing commitments, cutting principal, rate or fees, extending maturities, the pro rata sharing in 2.17(b) and (c), the voting definitions, and releasing all or substantially all guarantees or collateral. They do not protect against subordinating the liens or payment. Required Lenders is more than 50% of credit exposure and unused commitments. B-2 and B-3 are $959m, about 33% of the $2.9bn, so they cannot block a Required Lender amendment on their own; the revolver banks and B-4 holders together are about 67%. And section 2.10(d) allows individually negotiated discounted prepayments of term loans, so the company can buy out chosen lenders below par without sharing pro rata. That is the route by which B-2 and B-3 could be left behind. The revolver lenders in the March 2025 amendment are JPMorgan as agent, Bank of America, CoBank, Truist, Wells Fargo, U.S. Bank, TD, Citizens, Fifth Third and MUFG. Partly answered: Bloomberg reported on October 8 a roughly $1.25bn first-lien term loan from existing lenders and new investors to pay down secured debt, with no priming reported; the November 16 8-K says the company is still pursuing new debt financing. Creditor groups have organised across the stack since July, but none is named publicly. The notes were around 28 on October 2, before the missed coupon. The 10-Q does not name the swap counterparties and says either side can terminate. Still open: the security agreement, which is not publicly filed, so whether the Point stake is pledged; live revolver and convert prices; and the Point transfer terms.
