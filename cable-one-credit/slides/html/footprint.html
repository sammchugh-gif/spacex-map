18% MDU, 8% mobile vs 27% and 6% for the US. Closest peers: Vyve (MBI), Mediacom.

Passings ≈ homes. 1.27 vs 1.18 per location, ~8% over; Comcast and Charter ~21%. A peer $/passing undervalues Cable One per home by ~10%.

Fiber cuts both ways. Costly to overbuild unsubsidised; squarely in subsidised rural builds. ~60% already face gig rivals.

For a buyer, fewer apartments: lower churn, less bulk-deal risk, but lower density than Charter (27%).

Two panels from the same FCC and Census build. Panel A is the kind of home in each footprint: each operator's FCC cable and fiber locations are mapped to 2020 Census blocks, and the building mix comes from the American Community Survey, table B25024, by block group. Cable One is 74% single-family, 8% mobile homes and other, and 18% apartments, against a US average of 68%, 6% and 27%. Only Vyve, which is MBI, has fewer apartments. Astound is the other extreme at 52%. Panel B compares reported passings with Census homes, both per FCC location. Cable One reports 1.27 passings per location against 1.18 Census homes, so its passings run about 8% above homes, the gap being business sites, growth since 2020 and counting rules. Comcast, Optimum, Charter and Cox report 1.54 to 1.87 passings per location, 16 to 28% above their Census homes. WOW!, Breezeline and Astound report fewer passings than homes. Two uses for the pitch. First, Cable One's 2.8 million passings are close to a real home count, so applying a big cable company's dollar-per-passing to Cable One undervalues it per home by roughly 10%: each of their passings is fewer real homes. Second, a rural, single-family footprint is expensive for unsubsidised fiber to overbuild, but it is exactly where subsidised rural fiber goes, and management already says about 60% of passings face gig-capable competition. Fewer apartments also means less exposure to bulk deals and in-building fiber, and usually lower churn. For a buyer such as Charter, Cable One lowers density. Caveats: Census 2020 housing counts include vacant homes; the mix is applied by neighbourhood, not building; Optimum counts residential units only, and Mediacom discloses only over 3 million passings.
