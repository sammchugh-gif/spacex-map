Cable One · credit view · Sources: Q3 10-Q; Nov 16 8-K; Cablefax

The catalysts are dated and close together, which is what makes this tradeable now. The single risk that matters for the loans is the MBI cash. If the TRO is dissolved on Dec 4 and the company pays GTCR before filing, about $480m leaves the estate, and with a 3.5x Bear multiple the loans recover about 73, below their 83 price: that is the exit trigger. The second risk is a priming transaction. Because the company says it is negotiating a GTCR financing to address $2.45bn of obligations, I want to read the credit agreement's pro-rata and sacred-rights provisions and know whether CoBank, with roughly $1.1bn, can block a Required Lender vote. The decline risk is real for the notes, but B-4 at 83 only loses money if EV falls below about $1.6bn, about 3.1x on $520m of EBITDA. The priming risk is now evidenced, not theoretical. The credit agreement's sacred rights protect pro-rata sharing but do not list lien or payment subordination, and the company's June exchange offer already drafted a first-out and second-out structure. Its October plan with GTCR was agreed with all revolving lenders except CoBank, repays B-4 in full and adds a new $1.3bn loan of undisclosed ranking, so B-2 and B-3 holders are the ones who could be left behind. Unencumbered franchise value is now a low risk: Cable One, Inc. and all sixteen subsidiaries are loan parties pledging substantially all assets. Legacy franchises sit in Cable One, Inc. itself, acquired ones in subsidiaries, and one Hargray franchise expressly allows a mortgage to secure debt. Only the unfiled security agreement's excluded-asset list could still carve them out.
