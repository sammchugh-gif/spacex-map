The swaps pay fixed at about 2.6% against SOFR, so with SOFR higher they are in the money to Cable One: a $57m asset at Sep 30, saving about $13m a year in interest. In a default the mechanics run through the ISDA, which I have not read. Before a filing the counterparty can treat the notes default as a cross-default once it ripens and either terminate or suspend payments under Section 2(a)(iii); US courts have not let a counterparty withhold indefinitely after a bankruptcy, so in practice it terminates. Swaps are safe-harboured, so termination is allowed despite the stay. Because the swap is an asset to Cable One, the counterparty owes the termination value. If, as is typical, the counterparty is a lender whose hedge is secured under the credit agreement, it will set off that amount against its loan claim. For our trade that means at most about $57m, two points of secured cover, and it may never reach the estate as cash. Don't count the lost hedge twice: the termination value is the present value of the interest savings, so once the swaps are settled the company is economically whole. In Chapter 11, undersecured lenders get no post-petition interest, and at emergence the old debt and its hedges are replaced. Cable One can also terminate at its own election, which would turn the asset into roughly $57m of cash before a filing.
