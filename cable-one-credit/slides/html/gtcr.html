Separate the equity from the claim. The equity is worth roughly nothing: on about $160m of falling EBITDA and $938m of debt due November 2027, MBI's enterprise value only exceeds its debt above about 6x, and Cable One's own accounts value the 55% at about $1m. So nobody should pay for the equity. What has value is GTCR's legal claim: outside bankruptcy a court could order the close; inside it, rejection turns the claim into an unsecured one that dilutes the notes. GTCR is not desperate: it has already taken about $350m out, $250m upfront in December 2024 and $100m of new MBI debt passed through to MBI's owners. Its fallback in a Cable One bankruptcy is a contested unsecured claim, maybe $300m to $480m allowed at about 29% weighted recovery in reorganised paper, worth perhaps $50m to $150m today, plus control of an MBI whose organised lenders can take the keys. So the rational deal is a settlement of perhaps $100m to $200m of value, paid in paper or bundled with GTCR new money, in which Cable One releases the put and does not take the equity. If Cable One wants MBI's systems for the cost savings management has cited, it should buy them from MBI's lenders after they restructure. I found no evidence that GTCR holds MBI debt.
