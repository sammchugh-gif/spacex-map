Cable One · credit view · Sources: FCC BDC Dec-25 provider files (Nov 3, 2026 release), tech codes 70–72; TIGER/Line 2020; Census 2020 housing

This splits each operator's FCC cable and fiber locations by the housing density of their Census block group, and shows where T-Mobile, Verizon or AT&T report fixed wireless at the same location. Cable One is the most rural of the larger operators: 51% of its locations are in block groups with fewer than 500 homes per square mile and 23% under 100, against 34% and 18% for US homes, 40% and 18% for Charter, and 28% and 9% for Comcast. Only Vyve, which is MBI, and Mediacom are more rural. That cuts two ways. Fixed wireless is lighter: the carriers report 100/20 or faster fixed wireless at 37% of Cable One's locations, joint lowest with Vyve, against 43 to 52% for the urban operators, though some fixed wireless is offered at 81%. Satellite is the bigger rural risk. I use the low-density share as a proxy because Starlink sells capacity cell by cell and surcharges or waitlists congested cells, so it competes hardest where homes are sparse. For the credit, this supports valuing Cable One below urban cable peers, which the 3.5 to 6.0x range already does, and it is another reason not to pay for MBI, whose footprint is even more rural: 73% under 500 homes per square mile and 37% under 100. Caveats: fixed-wireless filings are carriers' claims of where they could install within ten business days, and satellite exposure is inferred from density, not measured.
